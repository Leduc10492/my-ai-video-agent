# Action and physical execution

Write visible action as a causal process, not an isolated verb or a checklist of physics terminology. Include only the physical properties that make the authored action readable, believable, or resistant to a demonstrated failure.

## Build an executable action chain

Use the minimum relevant stages:

`start state -> trigger -> initiation -> development -> contact or transfer -> follow-through or secondary response -> ending state`

Not every action needs every stage. Preserve the user's action order and emphasis. Do not add preparation, contact, reaction, or aftermath merely to make the description look complete.

Give each `SHOT` or `SEGMENT` a clear focus: a dominant change, coherent authored action chain, or intentional held state. A static shot needs no invented action. When actions actually compete for time, hands, camera attention, or space, identify that conflict under `director-review.md`; do not silently split or remove authored actions.

## Bind action to subjects, props, and surfaces

State who acts, the path of the action, what is contacted or transferred, and the resulting state. Keep critical prop ownership, hand state, orientation, and contact consistent before, during, and after the action.

Use a visible relationship instead of an abstract command:

```text
His right palm catches the door edge; the hinge resists, then the door swings inward and settles against the stop.
```

Do not describe a future or withheld action as an absence when the current visible state can be drawn. Replace `the camera has not yet been raised` with the current state, such as `the camera hangs at her right side`, unless actively withholding the gesture is itself readable performance.

## Select only relevant physics

Choose only properties that materially affect the shot:

- weight and load
- contact and collision
- acceleration, deceleration, and inertia
- friction and resistance
- balance and mass transfer
- follow-through and recovery
- deformation, vibration, or rebound
- material, cloth, hair, or particle delay
- adhesion, viscosity, flow, residue, or accumulation
- wind, gravity, or suspension response

Write cause and visible effect together. Do not list generic terms or automatically describe ordinary heel strike, toe push-off, cloth lag, hair lag, or body mechanics when they are not visually important.

## Common action classes

- **Carried prop:** contact point, arm load, wrist response, acceleration, deceleration, final ownership
- **Running, kicking, or impact:** ground contact, mass transfer, collision point, recovery, follow-through
- **Door or mechanism:** grip, latch state, hinge or mechanism resistance, travel, stopping force
- **Liquid:** source, adhesion, viscosity, gravity direction, spray or flow path, residue
- **Weather and particles:** source, wind direction, depth distribution, contact, accumulation, dissipation
- **Vehicle:** tire contact, body mass, suspension, braking or turning inertia, final heading
- **Fabric or hair:** delay only when driven by a visible turn, step, impact, or wind source

For emitted smoke, steam, breath, dust, sparks, or debris, bind `source -> initial direction -> temporary shape -> spread or dissipation`. Treat ambient haze as a spatial depth state rather than an emitted effect.

## Timing and cuts

Preserve authored time ranges. Treat timestamps as rhythm allocation unless editing an inspectable master. Do not invent frame-precise contact points.

At a continuity cut, preserve the current action phase, body momentum, prop state, material state, and environmental response. Do not restart an action merely because the camera setup changes. Follow the declared new states across authored time jumps, montage, and location changes.

## Action and physics check

- every action has a visible subject, path, and result
- the number of action units fits the interval and camera attention
- contact, prop ownership, hand state, and orientation remain coherent
- physical responses are caused by the current action rather than decorative
- emitted effects have a source and dissipation path
- continuity cuts inherit momentum and material state; authored discontinuities establish their specified new states
- the ending image records the completed or intentionally held state
