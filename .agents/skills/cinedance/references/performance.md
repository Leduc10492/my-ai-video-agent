# Observable performance

Translate authored emotional intent into camera-readable behavior. Keep performance inside the `SHOT` or `SEGMENT` where its trigger occurs; do not output a standalone `ACTING` or `ACTING TASK` section.

## Use a playable performance chain

Write:

`emotional direction when useful + trigger + one to three observable changes + held or resulting state`

Choose the clearest combination of gaze, blink, breath, brow, eyelids, mouth, jaw, hands, shoulders, posture, balance, or action tempo. Stop when the intended beat is readable.

Do not write hidden motive, backstory, interpretation, or internal theory unless it is converted into visible behavior. Avoid stacking micro-expressions that compete or produce exaggerated acting.

## Attach each turn to a trigger

Place a reaction after the visible or audible event that causes it. If a scene has several emotional turns, give each one its own trigger and observable result. Do not summarize the whole arc with one adjective.

When the trigger is audible, keep the visible reaction in the execution block and place only the synchronization to the named sound cue in `AUDIO`.

## Control restrained emotion

A contrast such as `on the verge of tears but holding them back` is useful only when grounded in visible cues: moisture collecting without falling, a caught breath, tightened lower lids, steady mouth, restrained blink, or a hand that briefly tenses and settles.

Describe the held result directly. Do not automatically add tears, trembling, clenched fists, lowered eyes, smiles, or dramatic breathing to communicate emotion.

## Dialogue, speaking, and listening

Keep the visible pre-speech trigger, posture, gaze, gesture, and non-vocal reaction in the execution block. Put the exact line, delivery, mouth movement, lip sync, listener silence, and audio-to-reaction timing only in `AUDIO` under `audio-dialogue.md`.

A listener may breathe, shift weight, blink, or make an authored silent mouth reaction without acquiring a spoken line or voice. Do not force all listeners' mouths closed or make every visible character perform a conspicuous action. In an important group, give each prominent subject one readable state; stillness may be that state.

## Control intensity and continuity

Match performance amplitude to shot size and visibility. A close-up can carry eyelid, breath, and mouth tension; a wide shot usually needs posture, tempo, distance, or a larger gesture. Do not rely on facial micro-detail the camera cannot read.

Across continuity cuts, preserve emotional progression and unfinished gestures. A reaction shot inherits its trigger and current emotional state. An authored time jump, montage, or other discontinuity may establish a different state; do not force a continuous gesture across it.

## Performance check

- every emotional turn has a trigger
- performance is visible at the current shot size
- one to three cues carry each beat without overacting
- restrained states describe what remains visibly held
- dialogue mechanics occur only in `AUDIO`
- listeners and background characters have readable but non-competing states
- continuity cuts preserve emotion and gestures; authored discontinuities follow the specified progression
