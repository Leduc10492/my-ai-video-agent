# Spatial blocking, composition, and continuity

Describe only the spatial relationships needed to read the action and preserve continuity. Do not turn every shot into a coordinate survey or force foreground, midground, and background into a composition that does not need them.

In COMPILE MODE, clarify the shot contract and apply authorized changes while preserving everything outside their scope. In DIRECT MODE, create only the minimum geography and coverage needed.

## Construct a drawable opening image

Select only the dimensions required by the shot:

1. useful shot size and subject scale or screen position
2. necessary foreground, midground, background, or landmark relationship
3. visible subject state and critical contact with a prop or surface
4. the minimum scene-defining visual carriers

A visual carrier is a concrete element that carries location, scale, story, color, material, or atmosphere: a doorway, horizon, wall edge, practical light, story prop, worn surface, smoke layer, moving fabric, or restrained background activity.

Bind adjectives to visible carriers. Prefer `warm light across rough concrete` to several abstract mood adjectives. Do not invent props, landmarks, people, or depth layers to satisfy a count.

## Control first-frame intent

Place first-frame occupancy and opening relationships inside the first `SHOT` or `SEGMENT`. Do not create a separate first-frame section unless the user or platform workflow requires one.

If a subject must be visible immediately, state the positive occupancy. Allow an empty or environment-first opening only when geography, suspense, reveal, transition, or authored rhythm calls for it. Add one local delayed-appearance boundary only after that failure occurs or is strongly likely.

## Use minimum sufficient blocking

For each important subject, select only the relevant controls:

- screen position or depth layer
- relationship to another subject or landmark
- torso, head, or movement direction
- gaze target
- destination
- hand or body contact with a critical prop

A clear physical relationship such as `one palm remains on the scorched hood` is stronger than several invented measurements. Use exact distances only when authored, visibly reference-derived, composition-critical, or correcting a demonstrated proximity failure.

## Separate body orientation and gaze

Treat torso direction, head direction, and eye line separately only when their difference matters. State an explicit gaze target for dialogue, reaction, or a known gaze failure. Do not add gaze locks when eyes are hidden, irrelevant, or already unambiguous.

Keep visible posture, gaze, pre-speech trigger, and non-vocal reaction in the execution block. Route speech, delivery, lip movement, listener silence, and sound synchronization to `AUDIO`.

## Preserve screen direction and camera side

Define camera side only when crossing it would reverse subject relationships, gaze, or movement. Distinguish `screen-left/screen-right` from a character's own left/right when confusion is possible.

Change a left/right relationship only through an explicit camera-side change, visible movement, or elapsed time. Do not flip geography merely because the shot size changes.

## Preserve state across cuts

A continuity cut within the same time and place inherits states not changed by the authored action. For an explicit time jump, montage, location change, or other transition, use the specified new states and preserve only the intended matching relationships. Do not invent a transition to excuse a continuity error.

Relevant states include:

- active subjects and character count
- geography and landmark distance
- body direction, gaze, and screen direction
- wardrobe and appearance state
- prop ownership, orientation, contact, and hand state
- dirt, damage, water, smoke, weather, and light direction
- action phase and emotional progression

For each new shot, restate only the inherited state needed to understand the cut and the relationships that change. Do not repeat a complete map in every block.

## Spatial and continuity check

- the opening frame can be sketched from the text
- every important subject occupies one readable place
- only necessary spatial dimensions are specified
- no unsupported distance or percentage has been invented
- body, gaze, and screen direction are clear where they matter
- critical prop contact and hand state remain coherent
- continuity cuts preserve physical and emotional state; authored discontinuities follow their declared new states
- no empty-frame, teleportation, flipping, or position guard is duplicated
